Overview

The rules and facts that actually get tested — nothing else.

Core definitions

  • Aldehyde = RCHO — one H on the carbonyl carbon.
  • Ketone = RR′CO — two organic groups on the carbonyl carbon.
  • Both share the carbonyl group, C=O, which governs their chemistry.
  • R and R′ may be aliphatic or aromatic.

Key differences

  • Oxidation: aldehydes oxidize easily; ketones resist oxidation.
  • Nucleophilic addition: aldehydes are usually more reactive than ketones (less steric hindrance, less electron donation).

Nomenclature rules

  • Common aldehyde names ← carboxylic acid name, -ic acid → -aldehyde.
  • IUPAC aldehyde: -e → -al; carbonyl carbon is always C-1.
  • C-2 (IUPAC) = alpha carbon (common name system).
  • Simplest ketone: acetone. Otherwise: two group names + "ketone".
  • Aryl-carbonyl ketone → suffix "-phenone".
  • IUPAC ketone: -e → -one; carbonyl gets the lowest possible number.

Physical properties

  • Polar C=O → higher boiling points than non-polar compounds of similar mass.
  • No O–H/N–H for self hydrogen-bonding → lower b.p. than comparable alcohols/acids.
  • Lower members (≤ ~5 carbons) are water-soluble via H-bonding with water.
  • All are soluble in the usual organic solvents.

Reducing agents

  • LiAlH4 and NaBH4 give the same product.
  • Aldehyde + [H] → 1° alcohol
  • Ketone + [H] → 2° alcohol

Grignard outcomes

  • Formaldehyde + RMgX → 1° alcohol
  • Other aldehyde + RMgX → 2° alcohol
  • Ketone + RMgX → 3° alcohol
  • Acid chlorides + RMgX overreact → also 3° alcohol (that's why organocadmium is used for clean ketone synthesis).

Oxidation & the Tollens' test

  • Aldehyde, acidic conditions → carboxylic acid.
  • Aldehyde, alkaline conditions → carboxylate salt.
  • Ketones do not oxidize this way (no H on carbonyl carbon).
  • Tollens' reagent (Ag(NH3)2+) → silver mirror only with aldehydes.

Carbon-nucleophile reactions

  • Aldol condensation: enolate + carbonyl → β-hydroxy aldehyde ("aldol"). One carbon always separates the aldehyde and alcohol carbons.
  • Claisen condensation: ester enolate + ester → β-ketoester (driven by the product's acidic α-H).
  • Cannizzaro: aldehydes with no α-H, in strong base → alcohol + carboxylate salt.
